TechMoms — Social Impact Challenge

Context

Helping mothers break into tech.

TechMoms is a nonprofit that provides accessible, community-driven tech education to mothers. Despite a powerful mission, their outreach struggled to reach the women who needed it most — the marketing lacked clarity, urgency, and visual consistency.

The Adobe Social Impact Challenge asked teams to use Adobe tools to make a measurable difference for a nonprofit. Our team chose TechMoms and set out to redesign their entire marketing strategy from the ground up.

Pitch deck — Mission slide

Problems

More programs, less visibility.

As TechMoms expanded — adding programs like Tech Moms in Color and Connectando Mamas en Tech — the outreach strategy couldn't keep up. The challenges were clear:

The Challenge

"How might we increase awareness of TechMoms' programs and drive meaningful enrollment across diverse, underserved communities?"

Strategy Pillars

Four ways to grow.

01

Market Programs & Alumni

Leverage alumni success stories and testimonials to make the value proposition tangible and emotionally compelling.

02

Expand Target Audiences

Reach underserved communities — including Latinas and women of color — with targeted programs and bilingual materials.

03

Develop New Programs

Pitch Connectando Mamas en Tech — an ESL-friendly, introductory tech program for non-English-speaking mothers.

04

Improve Website Effectiveness

Redesign the site to be more inviting, clarify calls-to-action, separate program applications, and reduce repetition — then build a live demo in Adobe Express to prove the concept.

Takeaways

What I learned.

01

Research first, design second. The most effective marketing decisions came from understanding TechMoms' existing alumni and their stories — data and testimonials shaped every design choice.

02

Inclusive design is intentional. Reaching Latina and underserved communities required more than translation — it meant rethinking channels, tone, imagery, and program structure entirely.

03

A live prototype wins arguments. Building the actual Adobe Express website — not just a mockup — let judges see the vision in action and gave our pitch undeniable credibility.
